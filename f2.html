The Global Crisis

When food is thrown out, the tragedy extends far beyond the lost calories. We waste the fresh water, agricultural land, massive amounts of energy, and human labor used to produce it.

Furthermore, rotting food in landfills is suffocating our planet. It undergoes anaerobic decomposition, generating methane—a greenhouse gas that is 25 times more potent than carbon dioxide at trapping heat in the atmosphere.

  • Food waste costs the global economy nearly $940 Billion each year.
  • If food waste were a country, it would be the 3rd largest emitter of greenhouse gases.
  • 40% of all food waste occurs precisely at the retail and consumer levels.

Our Founding Story

FoodShare started when a group of tech engineers and catering professionals witnessed the sheer volume of perfectly good, premium food being dumped into dumpsters after large corporate gala events in 2021.

Frustrated by the legal red tape and lack of immediate logistics to move this food to nearby shelters, they realized a fundamental truth: Hunger is not a problem of supply; it is a problem of logistics and real-time communication.

By leveraging modern mobile technology and geospatial algorithms, we created a digital bridge. Today, FoodShare serves as the vital link, ensuring that excess food meets empty plates in under 60 minutes.

The Process Explained

How FoodShare Works

We have engineered a flawless, real-time pipeline that removes all friction from food donation. From the kitchen to the community center in three easy steps.

1

Post Surplus Food

Using our mobile or web app, food donors quickly input details of their excess food. They select the food category (e.g., Prepared Meals), enter the quantity, and set a strict pick-up time.

2

Algorithmic Match

Our system instantly processes the donation and pings nearby, verified NGOs whose profiles match the food type and volume. The first NGO to accept the donation locks it in.

3

Secure Pickup & Track

A trained volunteer from the claiming NGO is dispatched. Once picked up, the food is tracked live until it is safely distributed, ensuring complete chain-of-custody transparency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a minimum amount of food required to donate?

No, there is no strict minimum. However, we recommend a minimum volume equivalent to 15 meals (approx. 15-20 lbs) to ensure the logistics are cost-effective and worthwhile for our NGO volunteer drivers.

What types of food can NOT be donated?

For safety reasons, we strictly prohibit: food that has been previously served or plated to a consumer, expired packaged goods, raw meat/seafood (unless frozen solid and packaged commercially), or unverified home-cooked meals.

How does the NGO verification process work?

Every NGO must pass a rigorous 5-point verification check. They must provide valid government non-profit registration documents (e.g., 501(c)(3)), pass a facility health inspection to ensure proper storage capabilities, and designate authorized personnel for pickups.

Is there a cost to use the platform?

For verified NGOs, the platform is 100% free forever. For enterprise donors, we offer a free base tier for local businesses, and a premium SaaS tier for large corporations requiring API integrations, multi-location dashboards, and custom ESG reporting tools.
